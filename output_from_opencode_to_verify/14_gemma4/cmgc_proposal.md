## Section 3: Project Understanding

The I-70 Floyd Hill to Veterans Memorial Tunnels project addresses one of Colorado's most critical transportation bottlenecks. This 8-mile stretch of I-70 through Clear Creek County features steep grades and dangerous curves that impede travel reliability, safety, and economic efficiency.

Our firm has performed an in-depth review of the project scope and has identified several primary technical and environmental challenges that will define the success of the construction phase:

**1. Segmental Bridge Construction in a High-Consequence Environment**
The project includes over 10,000 feet of new bridges, notably the massive 115-foot tall westbound bridge. The use of cast-in-place segmental construction with balanced cantilever methods requires exceptional precision. Executing these pours and segment placements while maintaining traffic flow on active I-70 lanes presents a significant operational risk that requires meticulous planning and specialized equipment.

**2. Massive Rock Excavation and Blasting in a Constrained Corridor**
The requirement for extensive rock scaling and blasting—with projections of over 600,000 tons of material removal—poses substantial risks to both the project schedule and the surrounding community. Managing blast vibration, controlling flyrock, and ensuring the safety of workers and the traveling public in the narrow corridor requires a sophisticated, technology-driven approach to blasting and muck hauling.

**3. Stringent Environmental Protections for Clear Creek**
Clear Creek is a vital ecological resource that runs through the heart of the project. We recognize the critical importance of protecting this watershed from construction-related impacts, such as sediment-laden runoff and chloride from winter maintenance. Our approach must strictly adhere to the requirements of the SWEEP ITF, the 1041 permit, and the FONSI mandates to prevent any degradation of water quality or habitat.

**4. Complexity of Concurrent Construction Phases**
The project is divided into East, Central, and West sections, with overlapping construction windows. Managing these three active work zones simultaneously—each with its own specific constraints, resource needs, and stakeholder impacts—requires advanced scheduling, coordination of material deliveries, and a highly organized approach to traffic management.

**5. Stakeholder and Community Impacts**
The project impacts a wide range of stakeholders, including Clear Creek County, the City of Idaho Springs, FHWA, and various environmental and community groups (e.g., UCCWA, I-70 Coalition). Successful delivery depends on our ability to maintain transparent communication, respond to community concerns, and minimize the impact on local residents and the regional economy.
