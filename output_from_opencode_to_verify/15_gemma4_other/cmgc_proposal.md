## Section 3: Project Understanding

The I-70 Floyd Hill project is a critical infrastructure upgrade addressing severe bottlenecks in the mountain corridor. The existing road conditions, characterized by a steep westbound climb of approximately 2,000 feet followed by a 600-foot descent in less than 2 miles, combined with tight curves rated for only 45 mph, create one of Colorado's most dangerous and unreliable segments for both passenger vehicles and heavy recreation traffic.

**Project Goals and Scope:**
The primary objective is to enhance safety and capacity by constructing a third westbound lane, functioning as a full-time Express Lane, stretching from Exit 247 to Exit 241. This involves a major highway realignment through Floyd Hill to straighten dangerous curves and increasing the design curve speed to 55 mph. The scope extends to over 10,000 feet of new bridge structure—including a new 115-foot tall bridge south into the hillside—and nearly 4 miles of new retaining walls requiring 73,000 square feet of shotcrete. Connectivity improvements include new roundabouts and a two-mile frontage road.

**Key Engineering and Environmental Constraints:**
The project is heavily influenced by significant geotechnical conditions, requiring extensive rock scaling and blasting throughout the corridor. In the East Section alone, 97,000 tons of material has already been removed. Furthermore, Clear Creek runs through the entire 8-mile project corridor, imposing stringent environmental controls. We acknowledge the NEPA FONSI status and the oversight of the SWEEP ITF, making water quality protection and sediment control paramount.

**Stakeholder and Operational Awareness:**
We recognize the complexity of managing construction across three concurrent geographic sections (East, Central, West) in a live, heavily trafficked corridor. Stakeholder engagement is broad, involving CDOT, the FHWA, Clear Creek County (1041 permit authority), and the Colorado Parks and Wildlife for wildlife crossing design (Genesee and Empire). Traffic management requires strict adherence to CDOT guidelines, including reducing speeds to 45 mph in work zones and implementing specific overnight closures for bridge work, while maintaining public access for recreational users.
